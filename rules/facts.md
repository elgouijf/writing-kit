# V. Facts, numbers and honesty

- **V1. Every number comes from a live source.** The data file, the repository, the measurement, the lecture slide. Never from memory, from a summary or derived notes, or from another sentence of the same text. Otherwise give no number. A derived note once gave a cumulative rate as a per-pass rate. (user)
- **V2. Check the author's own figures against the source too, and ask when they differ.** (user)
- **V3. Read the source before trusting any reviewer.** Reviewers and the author's own notes were both wrong at times: a capability declared missing that the history showed, a figure copied from a page that described it backwards, a saving true in cost but not in volume. (exp)
- **V4. Search everywhere before calling a source missing.** A truncated listing once hid three repositories. (exp)
- **V5. A frequency word needs a measurement.** "Often", "most" and "rarely" are claims about a number; measure them or drop them. (user)
- **V6. Never report a projection, or something never run, as a result.** Remove it; do not relabel it. (user)
- **V7. Never turn a measured limit into a capacity claim.** State the constraint and what was validated, nothing further, and do not add a to-do the author did not commit to. (user)
- **V8. Say what something guarantees, and no more.** Do not overclaim in objectives, results or conclusions; objectives and their verdicts must match what the time available could deliver. (review)
- **V9. A style pass must not add a fact.** After a rewrite, check every new noun against the source, not only the numbers. (exp)
- **V10. Evidence in a table or a conclusion comes from the body.** Never cite something the reader cannot find in the text. (user)
- **V11. Check which system or source a claim is about** before naming one: a component reads from the sources it actually reads, not from the one that sounds plausible. (user)
- **V12. Check the current state before describing a limitation.** A limitation must name something the reader has already met, and must still be true now. (user)
- **V13. A gap only the author can fill stays visible.** Print `\tofill{…}` in LaTeX or `**[TO FILL: …]**` in Markdown, and list it in the reply. A plausible invention is worse than a hole. (user)
- **V14. An unverifiable claim is written as the author states it, and flagged in the reply.** (user)
- **V15. Say how a finding was checked.** The verification is what makes a number credible. (user)
- **V16. Admit what was not measured in the sentence where the figure appears**, not in a separate disclaimer. (review)
- **V17. Describe work done by an AI tool as the tool's**, never as "by eye" or "human". (user)
- **V18. Show breadth with plain verbs, not with an inventory.** "They find a product by barcode, list products by type…" instead of a counted list of tools. (user)
- **V19. Before writing, collect what only the author knows.** For a report: organisation, subject, dates, supervisors, objectives as framed by the requester, existing solutions and constraints, what was built, tests and metrics, measured results, obstacles, and which parts are the author's own contribution. Keep the list in an `OPEN-QUESTIONS.md` and never invent an answer. (school)
- **V20. Confidential material stays out of anything shared.** Figures, customer data and design details from an employer stay in local repositories with no remote; mask names, customers and prices in screenshots; use demonstration data where real data is sensitive. (user)
