# S. Sentences, punctuation and connectors

- **S1. Vary sentence length.** Prose made only of medium-length sentences reads as generated. A short sentence is fine when it states a decision with a real actor (B9). (review)
- **S2. Split a sentence over about 45 words, and do not over-join.** A joined sentence over 45 words goes back to two. (user)
- **S3. Give a list of three or more items its own sentence**, the announcement first. (user)
- **S4. Vary the sentence that introduces a list.** "The main difficulties are the following" is right once; repeated at the head of every part it becomes a template, and so do announced counts ("We met four difficulties", "The audit had three difficulties"). Let bold lead-ins announce the items. (user)
- **S5. Use a colon only where nothing else fits.** A colon announcing an explanation becomes *because*, *since* or *so*; one announcing examples becomes *such as*; one announcing a list becomes its own sentence or an *of which* clause. Headings and "Top:/Bottom:" caption labels keep theirs. One pass took a report from 64 sentences with a colon to 7. (user)
- **S6. Cut the punctuation that does no work, and no more.** Drop the serial comma, the comma after a short opener, commas around short insertions and the appositive tail ("the nearest product, plausible and wrong"); join two sentences that share a subject. Keep the comma between two clauses and before a *which* that adds information. (user)
- **S7. A connector must state the real relation.** After a punctuation pass, read every new *since*, *as*, *so* and *which is why* aloud with both halves; an example is not a cause. When no connector fits, use a full stop. (user)
- **S8. Drop sloppy connective phrases.** "Measured in cost, …", "the two measurements that guided this work", "gave priority to": say it directly. (user)
- **S9. A proposed correction obeys the rules it enforces.** Write every suggested sentence as it would go into the text, never as a comma chain. (exp)
- **S10. Re-read a correction inside its paragraph.** A local fix that leaves an abrupt sentence or creates a repetition two lines later is not finished. (user)
- **S11. Less punctuation overall.** Chains of short sentences and comma-heavy lists fail a reader whatever the scans say. (user)
