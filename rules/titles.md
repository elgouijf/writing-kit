# T. Headings, captions and table headers

- **T1. A heading is a noun phrase and a technical statement.** "Insufficiency of name matching", "Proof of succession", "Criticality of false answers", "Stakes of recommendation". Never a question, a How/What/Why/When clause, a narration ("The engine we kept out") or an aphorism. (user, review)
- **T2. No leading gerund that narrates what the section does** ("Evaluating a recommender", "Understanding X"): use the noun ("Evaluation of recommender systems"). A gerund naming a trade-off is accepted when the noun form reads worse ("Balancing strictness and tolerance"). (user; settled in `conflicts.md`)
- **T3. A heading the author or a supervisor approved stays as approved**, even when it is a declarative sentence ("Collaboration required as much effort as the technical work", "One project consumed far more resources than the others"). (review)
- **T4. Headings are meaningful**: never a bare acronym, and specific enough to let a reader locate a piece of information. (school)
- **T5. The last section of a report or summary is titled "Conclusion"**, not "What I retain". (user)
- **T6. A caption is a noun phrase too, and so is its short form and every table header.** "How a risk line is rated" becomes "Rating of a risk line"; "When it is read" becomes "Loading". Grep `\caption`, short captions, table headers and bold text for How/What/Why/When before delivering. (user)
- **T7. Captions stay on one line, and what leaves a caption survives elsewhere**, in the text or the figure. A count the reader needs to read a chart stays. Captions under two side-by-side figures are the exception. (user)
- **T8. A register pass covers everything about its topic**: body, captions, figure labels, table headers, appendix and the paragraphs about it in other chapters. (user)
- **T9. Before delivering, grep every heading** for Why, What, How, When and a leading gerund. (user)
