# P. Document profiles

A profile fixes the choices that legitimately differ between genres. Choose one before writing, state it in the reply when it is not obvious, and let the project override any setting.

## P1. Engineering or internship report

- **Voice:** first person plural for what the team decided and did ("we compared ten configurations"); the tool or system as subject when it acts; present tense for how a system works. One way of naming the author throughout (W4). A school rule demanding "indirect style except for the personal contribution" is met by this: the contribution is stated once, in the first person, in one place (O9).
- **Length:** a page budget is a hard constraint (O13). Heavy material goes to appendices.
- **Examples:** one per point, only when it clarifies.
- **Modules:** all of F, V, R, W, S, B, O, T, G, X, C. Q before submission.
- **Template:** `templates/report/main.tex`.

## P2. Short academic report or assignment (one or two pages)

- **Voice:** impersonal: no "I", "we" or opinion section, even when the brief says "your investigation" (user). The AI-use statement is impersonal too.
- **Length:** respect the brief; if it says nothing, keep it short.
- **Headings:** noun phrases; the last section is "Conclusion" (user).
- **Modules:** F, V, R, W, S, B, O, T, C; G and X when it is LaTeX.
- **Template:** `templates/report/short.tex`.

## P3. Lecture summary handed in

- **Voice:** impersonal, third person (user).
- **Content:** state the ideas directly, never report on the lecture ("The lecture describes…") (user). One example per point at most, woven into a sentence (user). Synthesise instead of enumerating (user). Cover every section of the lecture.
- **Length:** when the brief says "longer is better", completeness comes first, but repetition still costs. A synthesis of a 90-minute lecture runs about 1 500 to 2 000 words; 3 900 words built from examples was rejected (user).
- **Modules:** V, R, W, S, B, O, T, C.

## P4. Academic English essay or course-graded writing

- **Voice:** impersonal unless the task asks for an opinion.
- **Genre rules usually taught by such courses:** no contractions, no "I think", no "lots of", no rhetorical questions, no "etc."; conclusions hedged in proportion to the evidence (B-row "calibrated hedging"); structure background → thesis statement → one topic sentence and its support per paragraph → summary and final comment. The course's own rules override this list.
- **Modules:** R, W, S, B, O, T, C.

## P5. Personal reflection or short answer that asks for feelings

- **Voice:** first person singular; the impersonal rule does not apply.
- **Content:** concrete, honest, short; no inflated claims of change.
- **Modules:** S, B, C (scan and read only).

## P6. Explanation to the user (chat, notes, course book)

- **Voice:** plain and direct; second person allowed in chat, impersonal in a course book.
- **Examples:** analogies and worked examples are required, not optional (E1); the one-example limit does not apply.
- **Modules:** E first, then R, S, B, T, G; X for a course book.

## P7. Professional writing (cover letter, e-mail, README, documentation)

- **Voice:** first person singular for letters and e-mails; imperative or impersonal for documentation.
- **Modules:** V, R, W, S, B; C reduced to the scan and a full read.
